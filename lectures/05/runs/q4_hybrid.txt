### Why RAG is Recommended Before Fine-Tuning for New Knowledge

* **Knowledge vs. Behavior:** Fine-tuning (FT) does not fix the knowledge problem (e.g., answering questions about specific facts or recent information depends on source materials that fine-tuning is unlikely to make reliably answerable) (Lecture 2, p. 53). Instead, fine-tuning is best used to change output format, tone, or behavior (Lecture 2, p. 53). 
* **Research Findings:** Research shows that on knowledge tasks, RAG consistently outperforms fine-tuning directly on documents (Ovadia et al., 2024), whereas trying to learn new knowledge via fine-tuning is slow and tends to increase errors or hallucinations (Gekhman et al., 2024) (Lecture 2, p. 47). 
* **Core Intervention Rule:** For a knowledge problem (such as dealing with a knowledge cutoff or new information), the course recommends providing the information using RAG or selecting a newer model rather than fine-tuning (Lecture 2, pp. 27, 47).

### How the Example Chatbot in Lecture 01 Uses RAG

The example chatbot searches lecture materials to answer questions and cites evidence (Lecture 1, p. 19). It operates in two main phases:

1. **Preparation (Making materials searchable):**
   * **Data collection:** Collects source materials like lecture notes (Lecture 1, p. 49).
   * **Filtering:** Removes unneeded documents (Lecture 1, p. 49).
   * **Chunking:** Splits the text into small pieces (Lecture 1, p. 49).
   * **Indexing:** Builds a search index so it is ready to search (Lecture 1, p. 49).

2. **Answering (Search, then answer):**
   * **Question:** Takes a student's question (Lecture 1, p. 49).
   * **Retriever:** Finds relevant materials in the index (Lecture 1, p. 49).
   * **LLM:** Generates an answer based on the retrieved materials and also displays the source materials/evidence (Lecture 1, pp. 19, 49).
