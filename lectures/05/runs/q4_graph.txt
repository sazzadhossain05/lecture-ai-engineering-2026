Based on the course materials from Lectures 01 and 02, here is how to think about this:

### 1. Why RAG is Recommended Over Fine-Tuning for Adding Knowledge
* **Nature of the Intervention:** According to Lecture 2, model capabilities and behaviors are structured in different stages [Lecture 2 · p.20]. Knowledge is fundamentally shaped during pre-training, whereas post-training (such as fine-tuning or prompts) primarily adjusts *how* the model answers (its tone, format, or behavior) [Lecture 2 · p.20, p.46].
* **Efficiency and Accuracy:** Research cited in the course (e.g., Ovadia et al., 2024; Gekhman et al., 2024) indicates that trying to memorize new facts or current documents through small-scale fine-tuning is inefficient, learned slowly, and tends to increase errors or hallucinations [Lecture 2 · p.46, p.47]. Therefore, for adding factual knowledge, providing information externally via RAG is the recommended first-line intervention [Lecture 2 · p.20, p.46].

### 2. How the Example Chatbot in Lecture 01 Uses RAG
In Lecture 1, the example chatbot follows a **"search, then answer"** approach split into two main phases [Lecture 1 · p.49]:
* **Preparation (Making materials searchable):** 
  1. *Data collection:* Gathers source documents (like lecture materials) [Lecture 1 · p.49].
  2. *Filtering:* Removes unneeded documents [Lecture 1 · p.49].
  3. *Chunking:* Splits the text into small pieces [Lecture 1 · p.49].
  4. *Indexing:* Builds a search index so it is ready for retrieval [Lecture 1 · p.49].
* **Answering (Retrieval and Generation):**
  1. *Question & Retriever:* When a student asks a question, a retriever searches the index to find relevant materials [Lecture 1 · p.49].
  2. *LLM Generation:* The Large Language Model generates an answer specifically based on the retrieved materials, and the chatbot also displays the source materials alongside the answer [Lecture 1 · p.49].
