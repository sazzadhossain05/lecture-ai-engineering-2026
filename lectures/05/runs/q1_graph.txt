Based on Lecture 02 (pages 51–52), here is how LoRA and QLoRA handle model weights:

* **What LoRA freezes:** The original base model weights ($W$), keeping them untouched (frozen) during training [lec02-main-p052].
* **What LoRA trains:** Only a small, low-rank change represented by two thin matrices (referred to as $A$ and $B$), which account for a tiny fraction of the parameters (often less than 1%) [lec02-main-p052].
* **What QLoRA adds:** QLoRA adds **quantization/compression of the original weights down to 4 bits** (a coarser numerical representation) [lec02-main-p052]. This further reduces the memory footprint of the base model (e.g., down to about 4 GB for a 7B model), making it possible to adapt very large foundation models on a single standard GPU [lec02-main-p052].
