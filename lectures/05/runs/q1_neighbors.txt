According to Lecture 2 (specifically pages 51 and 52), here is how LoRA and QLoRA divide what is frozen and what is trained:

* **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):**
  * **Freezes:** The original model weights ($W$), keeping them unchanged (represented as blue in the lecture diagrams).
  * **Trains:** Only a small added component—specifically, two thin matrices ($A$ and $B$) whose product represents the low-rank weight changes or "delta" (represented as orange). This trainable portion can be less than 1% of the total parameters.

* **QLoRA:**
  * **Adds:** Compresses the original base model weights to a coarser 4-bit numerical representation (storing them at 4 bits rather than 16 bits), which significantly reduces memory usage (e.g., down to about 4 GB for a 7-billion-parameter model).
