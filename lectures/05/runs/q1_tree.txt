In Lecture 02 (Section: *When and how to adapt the selected model*, page 52), the materials explain how LoRA and QLoRA work:

* **What LoRA freezes:** The original weights ($W$) of the pre-trained model are **frozen** and not trained (represented as the blue base model) (Lecture 2 · Japanese source p.52).
* **What LoRA trains:** It trains only small, low-rank adaptation matrices ($A$ and $B$, shown as orange/thin matrices) that represent the change (or delta) to the model, which can be less than 1% of the total parameters (Lecture 2 · Japanese source p.52).
* **What QLoRA adds:** QLoRA **quantizes and compresses the original frozen weights down to 4-bit precision** (a coarser numerical representation), which further reduces the memory footprint of the frozen weights to about one-fourth (e.g., down to roughly 4 GB for a 7-billion parameter model) while keeping the same LoRA training approach for the adaptation matrices (Lecture 2 · Japanese source p.52). 

To think about this further: why might compressing the frozen base weights to 4-bit precision via QLoRA be so important when fine-tuning very large foundation models (like a 65B model) on limited hardware such as a single GPU?
