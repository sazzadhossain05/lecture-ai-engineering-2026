According to Lecture 3 (Part 7, "Stop Conditions and Failure Behavior"), there are **three ways to stop an agent loop**:

1. **Model:** The loop ends when the model decides it is "done" (by stopping tool calls or ending its output). However, left alone, the model may stop too early or never stop.
2. **Harness:** The loop stops at a set limit enforced by the harness (such as repeated failures, or exceeding time or budget limits), rather than being self-reported by the model.
3. **Human / Monitor:** The loop is stopped from the outside—for example, when a person presses a stop button. The agent itself cannot turn this off.

---

### Who should decide whether the task is done?

Materials emphasize that **someone other than the working model** should decide if a task is truly done (Lecture 3, p. 43–44):
* **Relying solely on the working model is risky**, because models can falsely claim to be "done" even after failing or tampering with tests.
* Instead, completion should be decided by **external evidence and independent checks**, such as:
  * A **separate judge model** (e.g., using a smaller model to evaluate each turn's results).
  * A **fixed evaluation script** that the agent cannot modify (e.g., checking test results, diffs, logs, or state records rather than just taking the agent's word for it).
