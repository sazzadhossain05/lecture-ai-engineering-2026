To understand why a foundation model may struggle with questions about specific or up-to-date schedules (like a current course schedule or new information announced this year), think about how models generate text and handle knowledge:

* **Probabilistic Generation & Knowledge Cutoffs:** Lecture 2 notes that foundation models select next words probabilistically and have a knowledge cutoff. When asked about things outside their training data or specific current materials (such as specific course schedules or assignments), they do not necessarily answer "I don't know"; instead, they tend to generate plausible-sounding answers (hallucinations) that fit the context of the question (Lecture 2, p. 14, p. 27). 
* **The Role of Context / Information Limits:** As Lecture 2 also points out, simply letting a model "think more" (increasing inference-time compute) cannot produce information it does not possess (Lecture 2, p. 27).

To solve this limitation, Lecture 1 introduces an example chatbot that relies on external information rather than relying purely on the model's internal memory:

* **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation):** Lecture 1 discusses an example chatbot designed to handle lecture questions by using **RAG** (search, then answer). It searches the specific lecture materials/indices first and then has the LLM generate an answer directly based on the retrieved source documents (Lecture 1, p. 49).
