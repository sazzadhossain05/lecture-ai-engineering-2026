To answer your question based on the course materials:

* **What LoRA freezes and trains:** 
  In LoRA, the original weights of the model are frozen and not trained. Instead, it trains only small added matrices (adapters) represented by two thin matrices $A$ and $B$ (low-rank representation) [Lecture 2 · translated PDF p.75, Japanese source p.52].

* **What QLoRA adds:** 
  QLoRA builds upon LoRA by also compressing the original weights of the foundation model to **4 bits** (using coarse numerical representation like NF4) to further save memory. The adapters themselves are still trained at 16-bit precision [Lecture 2 · translated PDF p.75, Japanese source p.51, p.52, p.26]. 

**Tip for thinking further:** Think about why freezing the main weights and training only a tiny fraction of parameters (often less than 1% of the total) makes it possible to adapt a large model on a modest GPU like a Colab T4 [Lecture 2 · translated PDF p.75].
